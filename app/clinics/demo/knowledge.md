## Initial physiotherapy assessment (45 minutes, £65)

The initial assessment is your first appointment with the physiotherapist. It is the starting point for all new patients.

What happens during the assessment:
- The physiotherapist listens to your symptoms, goals, and medical history
- Physical examination: we assess your movement, strength, flexibility, posture, and functional ability
- The physiotherapist explains their findings in clear, simple terms
- Hands-on treatment is often provided in the same session where appropriate (soft tissue work, joint techniques, etc.)
- You leave with a clear treatment plan, home exercises, and agreed next steps

The assessment gives us a thorough understanding of the root cause of your problem, not just the symptoms. We do not guess — we assess.

This appointment is suitable for: first-time patients, new injuries, longstanding pain, post-surgical recovery, anyone unsure where to start.

---

## Follow-up physiotherapy appointments (30 minutes, £45)

Follow-up sessions build on the initial assessment and are where recovery really progresses.

Each follow-up typically includes:
- A check-in on how you have been since the last session
- Reassessment of your symptoms and function
- Hands-on treatment where appropriate
- Exercise progression and movement retraining
- Updates to your home exercise plan
- Discussion of activity guidance and self-management strategies

The physiotherapist adjusts the plan based on your response and goals. No two sessions are the same — your treatment evolves with you.

How many sessions will I need? This varies depending on the condition, its severity, your goals, and how you respond to treatment. The physiotherapist will discuss realistic expectations at the assessment and review progress regularly. Many patients see significant improvement in 3 to 6 sessions.

## Sports massage (30 or 60 minutes)

Sports massage uses hands-on soft tissue techniques to reduce muscle tension, improve movement, and support recovery.

It is beneficial for:
- People who train or exercise regularly
- Muscular tightness from work or daily life
- Recovery support alongside physiotherapy
- Managing muscle soreness or stiffness
- Pre-event preparation and post-event recovery

The therapist will discuss your needs and comfort level before and during treatment. Sports massage can be provided as a standalone treatment or alongside physiotherapy.

Prices: £40 for 30 minutes, £70 for 60 minutes.

## Shockwave therapy (£55 per session)

Shockwave therapy uses targeted acoustic waves to stimulate healing in persistent tendon and soft tissue conditions.

It is particularly effective when symptoms have not improved with standard physiotherapy and exercise alone.

Common conditions treated with shockwave:
- Plantar fasciitis (heel pain)
- Achilles tendonitis
- Tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis)
- Golfer's elbow (medial epicondylitis)
- Rotator cuff tendon problems
- Patellar tendonitis (jumper's knee)
- Calcific shoulder tendinopathy

What to expect: The physiotherapist will assess whether shockwave is appropriate and safe for you. During treatment, the device is placed against the skin over the affected area. It can feel intense on the sore area, but sessions are short (typically 5 to 10 minutes). Most patients notice improvement after 3 to 6 sessions. Mild soreness for 24 to 48 hours after treatment is normal.

Shockwave is not suitable for everyone. The physiotherapist will confirm suitability before starting.

## Rehabilitation and strength programmes

Rehabilitation programmes help patients return to normal activity, sport, or work safely and with confidence.

Programmes may include:
- Progressive strengthening and loading plans
- Movement control and technique coaching
- Functional rehabilitation aligned to your goals (sport, work, daily life)
- Return-to-running or return-to-sport progressions where appropriate
- Balance and proprioception training

Programmes are always adjusted based on progress and feedback. They are especially important after surgery or a serious injury, where structured, expert guidance is essential to rebuild safely.

## Conditions we treat

Roch Physio Clinic treats a wide range of musculoskeletal and soft tissue conditions.

BACK PAIN

Lower back pain is one of the most common conditions we treat. Whether it is acute or longstanding, we identify the cause and create a plan to reduce pain, improve movement, and prevent recurrence. Physiotherapy is highly effective for lower back pain.

Upper back and thoracic pain — often related to posture, desk work, or muscle tension — responds well to physiotherapy and soft tissue treatment.

Sciatica and nerve pain refer pain radiating from the back down into the leg, sometimes accompanied by tingling or numbness. Physiotherapy can reduce nerve irritation, relieve the pain, and address the underlying cause.

Disc problems including herniated, bulging, or prolapsed discs can be treated effectively with physiotherapy in many cases. We focus on reducing pressure, improving movement, and strengthening the supporting muscles.

---

NECK PAIN

Neck pain from poor posture, muscle tension, or joint stiffness responds well to physiotherapy. We assess the cause and work on restoring movement and strength.

Whiplash injuries from road traffic accidents need proper assessment and treatment to heal correctly. Physiotherapy helps restore normal movement, reduce pain, and prevent chronic problems.

Headaches related to neck tension and posture are very common and often go undiagnosed. Physiotherapy can make a significant difference for tension headaches and cervicogenic headaches (headaches coming from the neck).

---

SHOULDER PAIN

Rotator cuff injuries, tendon pain, and impingement syndromes are among the most common shoulder problems we see. Physiotherapy, combined with shockwave therapy where appropriate, is very effective.

Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis) is a condition causing progressive stiffness and pain in the shoulder joint. Physiotherapy uses specific techniques to gradually restore movement.

Shoulder instability and post-surgical shoulder rehabilitation are also commonly treated.

---

ELBOW, WRIST, HAND

Tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis) — pain on the outside of the elbow — is one of the best conditions for shockwave therapy. Physiotherapy combined with shockwave has excellent results.

Golfer's elbow (medial epicondylitis) — pain on the inside of the elbow — is treated similarly.

Wrist pain and carpal tunnel syndrome causing tingling, numbness, or weakness in the hand respond well to physiotherapy. Laser or shockwave therapy may also be used.

---

HIP AND GROIN

Hip pain from the joint, labrum, tendons, or surrounding muscles. Physiotherapy assesses the true cause and works on movement, strength, and function.

Hip flexor pain and groin injuries are common in active patients and runners.

Greater trochanteric pain syndrome (GTPS) — pain on the outer hip — is a tendon-related condition that responds well to physiotherapy and loading programmes.

---

KNEE PAIN

Patellofemoral pain (runner's knee) — pain at the front of the knee — is very common in runners, cyclists, and people who do a lot of stairs or squats. Physiotherapy addresses the biomechanical causes.

Meniscus injuries can often be managed very effectively with physiotherapy, sometimes without surgery.

IT band syndrome (iliotibial band syndrome) — pain on the outer knee — is common in runners and cyclists and responds well to physiotherapy and strength work.

Knee ligament injuries including partial ACL, MCL, and PCL injuries are treated with rehabilitation programmes.

Knee tendon pain including patellar tendonitis (jumper's knee) responds well to loading programmes and shockwave therapy.

Osteoarthritis of the knee — physiotherapy can significantly improve pain and function even when cartilage is worn.

---

ANKLE AND FOOT

Ankle sprains — whether recent or recurrent — need proper rehabilitation to heal correctly and prevent chronic instability. We restore strength, stability, and proprioception.

Achilles tendonitis and Achilles tendinopathy respond well to physiotherapy and shockwave therapy. A graded loading programme is key to recovery.

Plantar fasciitis (heel pain, especially in the morning) — shockwave therapy has excellent results. Physiotherapy addresses the underlying tightness and weakness.

Calf strains and posterior tibialis tendon issues are also treated.

---

SPORTS INJURIES

We treat athletes and active individuals across all sports including football, rugby, cricket, running, cycling, swimming, golf, racquet sports, and gym training.

Sports injuries need expert assessment to understand the full extent and create a safe return-to-sport plan. We work with you to get back to doing what you love, as quickly and safely as possible.

---

POST-SURGICAL REHABILITATION

We provide physiotherapy after a wide range of orthopaedic surgeries including:
- Knee replacements
- Hip replacements
- ACL reconstruction
- Shoulder surgery (rotator cuff repair, stabilisation, labrum repair)
- Ankle surgery
- Spinal surgery
- Wrist and hand surgery

Early post-surgical physiotherapy significantly improves outcomes. We work with your surgical team where appropriate.

---

CHRONIC PAIN AND LONGSTANDING CONDITIONS

Chronic pain — pain lasting more than 3 months — often needs a different approach from acute injuries. We understand the physical and psychological dimensions of chronic pain and take a holistic view.

Conditions including fibromyalgia, chronic lower back pain, arthritis, and widespread pain can all benefit from physiotherapy.

---

WORK-RELATED AND POSTURE-RELATED PAIN

Pain from desk work, repetitive strain injuries (RSI), and poor ergonomics are extremely common. Physiotherapy can treat the current problem and give you the tools to prevent recurrence with postural correction, ergonomic advice, and strengthening exercises.

## Safety and medical emergencies

The receptionist is not a clinician and cannot diagnose conditions by phone. All clinical advice comes from your physiotherapist during your appointment.

If your symptoms are severe, rapidly worsening, or you have urgent concerns:
- For urgent medical advice in the UK, contact NHS 111
- In an emergency, call 999 or attend your nearest A&E

Seek emergency help immediately if you experience: severe chest pain, difficulty breathing, signs of a stroke (facial drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty), loss of bladder or bowel control alongside back pain, or any other symptoms that feel urgent or life-threatening.
