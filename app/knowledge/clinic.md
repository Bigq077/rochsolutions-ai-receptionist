## Initial physiotherapy assessment (45 minutes)

An initial assessment is the first appointment with the clinician.

Typical structure:
- History taking: symptoms, goals, medical history, lifestyle factors
- Physical examination: movement, strength, flexibility, posture, functional tests
- Explanation of findings in clear terms
- Hands-on treatment if appropriate (e.g., soft tissue or joint techniques)
- Plan: exercises, activity guidance, and next steps

What to wear:
- Comfortable clothing (shorts for knee/ankle, vest/t-shirt for shoulder/neck when possible)

Bring if available:
- Scans, reports, referral letters
- Insurance details (if using insurance)

---

## Follow-up physiotherapy appointments (30 minutes)

Follow-ups build on the initial assessment and focus on progress.

They may include:
- Reassessment of symptoms and function
- Hands-on treatment where appropriate
- Exercise progression and movement retraining
- Education and self-management strategies
- Updated plan to reduce recurrence and improve performance

The clinician adjusts the plan based on response and goals.

---

## Sports massage (30 or 60 minutes)

Sports massage focuses on soft tissue techniques to reduce muscle tension, improve movement, and support recovery.

It can be helpful for:
- People who train regularly
- Muscular tightness from work or daily life
- Recovery support alongside physiotherapy
- Managing stiffness or soreness

The therapist will discuss suitability and comfort before and during treatment.

---

## Shockwave therapy

Shockwave therapy uses targeted acoustic waves to stimulate tissue healing in certain persistent tendon and soft tissue conditions.

Common reasons it is considered:
- Symptoms not improving with standard conservative care
- Tendon-related pain (suitability assessed case-by-case)

What to expect:
- A clinician assesses if it is appropriate and safe
- The clinician explains sessions needed and expected response
- Some short-term discomfort can occur during treatment
- Patients are advised about aftercare and activity guidance

Shockwave is not suitable for everyone. The clinic will confirm suitability before starting.

## Rehabilitation and strength programmes

Rehab programmes help patients return to normal activity, sport, or work safely and confidently.

They may include:
- Progressive strengthening and loading plans
- Movement control work and technique coaching
- Functional rehab aligned to goals (sport/work/daily life)
- Return-to-running or return-to-sport progressions where appropriate

Programmes are adjusted based on progress and feedback.

RochSolutions Clinic commonly sees:

- Knee pain (running-related pain, ligament/tendon irritation, patellofemoral pain)

- Post-operative rehabilitation
- Work-related or posture-related issues

If a patient is unsure whether their issue is suitable, the clinic can advise.

## Do I need a referral?

A GP or consultant referral is not usually required for private physiotherapy.
However, some insurance providers may require a referral depending on policy terms.

If using insurance, it is best to check directly with the provider.

## How many sessions will I need?

This varies depending on:
- The condition and severity
- Goals and timelines
- Response to treatment
- Consistency with home exercise and activity guidance

The physiotherapist will discuss expectations and review progress regularly.

## Can I exercise or work while having treatment?

It depends on the condition, irritability of symptoms, and the plan.
The clinician will provide personalised guidance on:
- Training modifications
- Work adjustments
- Safe activity levels during recovery
