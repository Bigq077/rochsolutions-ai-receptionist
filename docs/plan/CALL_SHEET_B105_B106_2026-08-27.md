## Test A — B-105 · a day picked by its number

**Setup:** you need a **numbered readout covering TWO OR MORE DAYS**. Ask
broadly; do not name a day.

| | Say |
|---|---|
| 1 | "I'd like to book a physio assessment" |
| 2 | *(answer whatever she asks — reason, location for Theorem)* |
| 3 | **"what have you got next week"** |
| 4 | *(she reads a numbered list — write down which day is* **Number 2**) |
| 5 | **"the second day suits me, could you give me all the slots you have on that day"** |

Step 5 is verbatim from `CA0eb9a12c` at 13:59:08.

**PASS** — she answers about the day that was **Number 2**, and the log carries:

```
[slot_followup] scoping the follow-up to 2026-09-08 -- the caller picked it by position in the readout, and the offer on the table leads with 2026-09-07 (B-105)
```

Note the two dates *disagree*. That is the fix working: the offer leads with day
one, the caller meant day two, and the scope followed the caller.

**FAIL** — she answers about **Number 1**'s day, under Number 1's label.

> **The failure is silent.** The old path logs *nothing at all* — it just
> defaults to `last_offered_slots[0]`. So you cannot grep for the bug. **Absence
> of the `(B-105)` line with a positional pick on the transcript is the failure.**

**If it fails,** capture the SID and check whether she offered one day or two. A
single-day readout cannot exercise this — redial and ask more broadly.

## Test B — B-106 · a bare date

**Setup:** get her to offer times on **one specific day**, then ask about a
different date **by number only** — no weekday, no month.

| | Say |
|---|---|
| 1 | "I'd like to book a physio assessment" |
| 2 | *(answer whatever she asks)* |
| 3 | **"have you got anything on wednesday by any chance"** |
| 4 | *(she offers times on a Wednesday — note its date)* |
| 5 | **"what about the 22nd"** |

Steps 3 and 5 are verbatim from `CA8c4efedb` at 13:53 and 13:54:31.

**Choosing the date at step 5 — this matters:**
- Its **day-of-month must differ from every day she has offered.** If she has
  already offered the 22nd, say a different number.
- Pick something **2–4 weeks out** that plausibly has availability, so a false
  "fully booked" is actually detectable.
- **Use a number 4 or higher.** "the second" / "the third" are deliberately not
  read as dates — they collide with picking option 2 off a list. That is by
  design, not a bug to report.

**PASS** — she does **not** claim the day is booked, and the log carries:

```
[ms_llm] caller named a date that is on no offered day -- treating as a different-day request (B-106)
```

followed by a real `check_availability` call.

**FAIL** — either of these:
- she says anything of the shape *"the 22nd is fully booked"* / *"I don't have
  anything on the 22nd"*, **or**
- the log carries this instead of the `(B-106)` line:

```
[ms_llm] check_availability BLOCKED — slots already retrieved this turn
```

That BLOCKED line appearing **right after the caller names a date** is the
defect, verbatim. (It is legitimate after "is that all you have?" — the trigger
is what the caller just said.)

### What PASS does *not* mean
She will run a lookup **for the week containing the 22nd**, not for the 22nd
itself. **No branch has exact-date filtering** — `date_hint` drives a week range
and the filter is weekday + time-of-day. So being offered the 24th when you asked
for the 22nd is **expected and correct**. The bug was inventing "fully booked";
it was never that she can pin an exact date. Do not raise that as a failure.
