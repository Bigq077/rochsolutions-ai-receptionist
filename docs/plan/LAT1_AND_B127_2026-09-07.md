## 2. LAT-1 — the part-of-day suffix is now a clinic decision

`operational.speak_part_of_day`, **default true**. Every clinic keeps the
wording it has until its own `clinic.json` opts out, because a change to what
patients hear belongs to the clinic and not to the engine.

**Off for `northgate` only** — the demo line, which exists to be listened to.

### Measured, at the 3.82 words/sec CA8d5b2e3e actually ran at

| | this morning | after the opener+tail fix | after the suffix drop |
|---|---|---|---|
| single-day | 47 w · **12.7 s** · 247 ch | 39 w · 10.2 s · 197 ch | 30 w · **7.9 s** · 150 ch |
| multi-day | 66 w · **17.3 s** | 66 w · 17.3 s | 48 w · **12.6 s** |

Demo line: single-day **−38%**, multi-day **−27%**.
Patient clinics: the opener and tail fix only, so 12.7 s → 10.2 s.

### Why the drop is safe, measured rather than argued

* over **236 real day-offers** carrying **2262 spoken labels**, dropping the
  suffix created **zero** pairs that sound alike;
* **no clinic offers an hour together with its twelve-hour twin** — spreads are
  northgate 08–18 and theorem 09–18, both narrower than twelve hours, so a
  clock face names exactly one time inside a working day;
* `slot_accepted_by_caller` has **always** stripped the suffix before matching,
  so nothing a caller may *say* changes — the tests assert a pick resolving both
  from what they now hear ("ten to nine") and from the long form they might say
  out of habit;
* `_time_contradicts` reads the START time and never the label, so
  "number two in the morning" against a 16:20 slot is still refused.

### What is NOT measurable, and why it is a flag

The suffix also **confirms** to a caller that "eight" is the morning. No corpus
can price that. Hence: off on the line that exists for listening to, on
everywhere a patient might ring.

### Containment

Threaded from each availability path's **own** session across all seven
`_build_days_data` call sites. The parameter defaults true, so a path that
forgets it does not crash — it silently gives that clinic the other wording on
one reader and not another. A structural test walks every call site for exactly
that.

## 3. The multi-day opener follows the single-day rule now

"Here's what we've got coming up —" reads as the diary's upcoming days, and it
was spoken whatever the sweep held behind it.

multi_day has **no counterweight**: B-99 suppresses the "a few others that day"
tail here, correctly, because "that day" has no referent once three days are
named. So that sentence was the only thing the caller had, and it was the
confident version. With a day held back it is now "I've got a few days —".
